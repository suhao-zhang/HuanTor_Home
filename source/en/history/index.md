<div class="section-head section-head--left">
  <h1>Only dates we can point to</h1>
  <p>Nothing here is filled in with an unverifiable founding year or a client count</p>
</div>

<div class="about__text">
  <p>Most company timelines run from a founding year to "recognised by the industry". This one is different by choice: an entry appears only if it can be checked in a public record — a filing number, a domain, a deployment log, published content. Everything else is described as capability rather than as a date.</p>
</div>

<div class="section-head" style="margin-top: 48px;">
  <h2>Verifiable entries</h2>
  <p>Three, each checkable outside this website</p>
</div>

<div class="ht-timeline ht-stagger">
  <div class="ht-step">
    <div class="ht-step__head"><h3>ICP filing completed</h3><span class="ht-step__tag">within 2025</span></div>
    <p>The domain huant.ltd received the filing number <strong>豫ICP备2025140588号</strong>. It is shown in the footer of every page on this site and links to the MIIT filing lookup, where it can be checked against the company name.</p>
    <p class="ht-step__out"><b>Check at:</b> the MIIT ICP filing and licence enquiry system</p>
  </div>
  <div class="ht-step">
    <div class="ht-step__head"><h3>Website relaunched</h3><span class="ht-step__tag">October 2026</span></div>
    <p>The public positioning was narrowed to one line: AI engineering delivery. Large-model applications, industrial AI, software and SaaS systems, cloud deployment and operations, and GEO. Home, company, product and contact pages were rewritten to that scope.</p>
    <p class="ht-step__out"><b>Check at:</b> domain and deployment records, first publication dates of the pages</p>
  </div>
  <div class="ht-step">
    <div class="ht-step__head"><h3>Technical library kept current</h3><span class="ht-step__tag">ongoing</span></div>
    <p>More than five hundred articles on AI engineering, cloud services, back-end architecture, industrial AI and policy interpretation, each with its sources named so the judgement can be re-checked. It is how we explain our engineering practice in public, and it is the content layer behind GEO work.</p>
    <p class="ht-step__out"><b>Check at:</b> the news list and the site archive (Chinese)</p>
  </div>
</div>

<div class="section-head" style="margin-top: 72px;">
  <h2>How the capability developed</h2>
  <p>Four stages without dates, because the order matters more than the calendar</p>
</div>

<div class="ht-timeline ht-stagger">
  <div class="ht-step">
    <div class="ht-step__head"><h3>First: deliver a tool</h3><span class="ht-step__tag">scripts and small systems</span></div>
    <p>Turn one repetitive job into something usable: a batch script, a dashboard, a box for looking things up. This stage teaches asking what is needed, shipping fast, and having someone actually use it.</p>
  </div>
  <div class="ht-step">
    <div class="ht-step__head"><h3>Then: deliver a system</h3><span class="ht-step__tag">permissions and integration</span></div>
    <p>A tool becomes a system once it has accounts and roles, multi-step processes, integration with what already exists, and decisions about storage and migration. This stage teaches engineering constraints, not feature counts.</p>
  </div>
  <div class="ht-step">
    <div class="ht-step__head"><h3>Then: own the model and data chain</h3><span class="ht-step__tag">quality you can accept</span></div>
    <p>From calling a ready-made API to deciding whether to fine-tune or fix the retrieval instead, how to define an evaluation set, and which kind of failure a bad answer belongs to. Only after this can a quality level be committed to.</p>
  </div>
  <div class="ht-step">
    <div class="ht-step__head"><h3>Finally: operate it long-term</h3><span class="ht-step__tag">data flows back</span></div>
    <p>Go-live is the start: dashboards, alerting, knowledge-base upkeep, release cadence, GEO monitoring. Keeping something running is what turns a project into a product.</p>
  </div>
</div>
